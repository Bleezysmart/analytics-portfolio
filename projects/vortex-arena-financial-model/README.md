# Vortex Arena Ltd. — Five-Year Financial Model

**Financial Modelling Case Study | 2027–2031 | Lagos, Nigeria**

## Project overview
Vortex Arena Ltd. is a fictional paintball and recreation business developed as a financial-modelling capstone. The project translates operating assumptions into a five-year integrated financial model to evaluate projected profitability, financial position, cash generation and sensitivity to changes in business conditions.

## Business model and operating assumptions
- One paintball arena in Lagos.
- 16 players per session and four sessions per day.
- 312 operating days per year, giving theoretical annual capacity of 19,968 player visits.
- Standard package price of ₦12,000 per player in 2027, with 10% annual price escalation.
- Base Case occupancy increases from 30% in 2027 to 50% in 2031.
- Initial equity funding assumption of ₦60 million; no debt financing in the base funding plan.
- Initial capital expenditure of approximately ₦24.69 million.

| Closing cash balance | ₦44.1m | ₦223.2m |

These are model projections, not actual operating results. Outcomes depend on the assumptions and execution.

## Scenario analysis
The model includes three operating scenarios:
- **Worst Case:** revenue index 80%; cost index 120%.
- **Base Case:** revenue index 100%; cost index 100%.
- **Best Case:** revenue index 120%; cost index 80%.

The scenario framework tests how changes in demand and cost conditions affect projected performance.

## Key analytical considerations
- Occupancy and customer demand are central revenue drivers.
- Consumables, payroll, rent and other operating expenses influence margins.
- Cash generation and the Balance Sheet are reviewed alongside profitability.
- The scenario analysis highlights downside and upside sensitivity.

## Decision questions the model is designed to support
- What revenue is implied by the arena's capacity, pricing and occupancy assumptions?
- How sensitive are profitability and cash generation to weaker demand or higher costs?
- Do projected earnings translate into cash, and how does the financial position evolve over the forecast period?
- What does the base case indicate about the economics of operating one arena?

The model provides a structured way to test these questions. It does not establish that the business will achieve the forecast, and it does not prove that expansion is viable.

## Model review and interpretation
The analysis connects the operating assumptions to the revenue build-up, expenses, projected financial statements and scenario outputs. Profitability is considered alongside closing cash and the projected balance sheet so that the assessment is not based on revenue or EBITDA alone.

**Interpretation boundary:** all headline outputs are forecasts for a fictional business. They depend on the stated assumptions and should be treated as model outputs rather than observed performance or a funding recommendation.
