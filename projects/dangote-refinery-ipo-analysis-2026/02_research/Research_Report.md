# Dangote Refinery IPO Analysis 2026

## Executive summary

The Dangote Petroleum Refinery & Petrochemicals IPO is assessed at an offer price of **₦525 per share** using reported data through H1 2026. The research question is whether the earnings power implied by that price can be supported under a range of operating and valuation assumptions.

H1 2026 shows a major operating turnaround. Revenue reached **$13.91B**, net profit **$1.82B**, and EBITDA / operating earnings were reconstructed at approximately **$2.60B**. Refinery utilization increased from **34.6% in FY2024** to **63.1% in FY2025** and **83.6% in H1 2026**. Refining margin increased from **$10.70/bbl** to **$13.70/bbl** and then **$24.50/bbl**.

The valuation framework uses a deliberately explicit **10× EV/EBITDA assumption**. With post-IPO equity value of approximately **$47.82B**, business value of approximately **$49.22B** after adding net debt, the framework requires roughly **$4.92B** of sustainable operating earnings. Annualizing H1 2026 operating earnings gives approximately **$5.20B**, leaving only about **$0.28B** of headroom. That annualized figure is a run-rate rather than a forecast.

The scenario model produces implied share values from **₦314 to ₦588**, with a base scenario of **₦446**. The model therefore focuses attention on the sustainability of H1 2026 margins and utilization, the large remaining expansion programme, funding requirements, taxes, FX and the valuation multiple applied to normalized earnings.

## 1. Approach

The project separates four categories:

1. Reported company and market data.
2. Analyst calculations derived from reported data.
3. Explicit valuation assumptions.
4. Scenario outputs and risk interpretation.

The objective is not to prove that the IPO is attractive or unattractive. The objective is to make the assumptions required to justify the price visible and testable.

## 2. IPO and capital structure

- Offer price: **₦525/share**
- New shares: **4.10B**
- Pre-IPO shares: **120.129B**
- Post-IPO shares: **~124.229B**
- Gross offer proceeds: **~₦2.153T**
- Net IPO proceeds: **~₦2.111T**
- Post-IPO equity value used in the model: **~$47.82B**
- Net debt: **~$1.40B**
- Business value: **~$49.22B**

The offer is treated as primary capital raised by the company for the stated expansion programme.

## 3. Operating performance

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue, USD bn | 6.34 | 12.33 | 13.91 |
| Throughput, bpd | ~225K | ~410K | ~585K |
| Utilization | 34.6% | 63.1% | 83.6% |
| Refining margin, USD/bbl | 10.70 | 13.70 | 24.50 |

H1 2026 revenue already exceeded FY2025 full-year revenue. Profitability also moved from losses in FY2024 and FY2025 to positive H1 2026 earnings.

## 4. Profitability and cash flow

H1 2026 gross profit was approximately **$2.50B**, operating profit approximately **$2.37B**, and net profit approximately **$1.82B**. Net margin was approximately **13.1%**, versus negative net margins in FY2024 and FY2025.

Operating cash flow was approximately **$1.27B** in the main model. A separate reporting extract showed a higher H1 2026 operating cash-flow figure; this was retained as a reconciliation issue rather than silently replacing the main historical series.

## 6. Peer benchmarking

The peer comparison covers major global refining companies including Valero Energy, Marathon Petroleum, Phillips 66, HF Sinclair and Tüpraş.

The model compares utilization and company-reported refining-margin measures while explicitly noting that the margin definitions are **not perfectly identical across companies**. This means the peer margin chart is a benchmark rather than a strict like-for-like accounting comparison.

Dangote's H1 2026 utilization of **83.6%** is below the utilization reported by the selected mature peers, while its **$24.50/bbl** refining margin is materially higher than the peer benchmark values used. This makes margin normalization an important sustainability question.

## 7. Growth and funding

The expansion programme is approximately **$14.30B**, with approximately **$11.80B** still required across H2 2026 through 2028:

| Period | Remaining expansion spending |
|---|---:|
| H2 2026 | $4.80B |
| 2027 | $3.90B |
| 2028 | $3.10B |
| **Total** | **$11.80B** |

H1 2026 cash was approximately **$4.27B** against gross debt of approximately **$5.67B**, producing net debt of approximately **$1.40B**.

## 8. Valuation test

At ₦525, the model asks how much sustainable operating earnings are required to support the implied business value under a 10× EV/EBITDA framework.

- Business value: **~$49.22B**
- Assumed EV/EBITDA: **10.0×**
- Required sustainable operating earnings: **~$4.92B**
- Annualized H1 2026 operating earnings: **~$5.20B**
- Headroom: **~$0.28B**

The small headroom means that the valuation is sensitive to normalization in margins, throughput, taxes, financing costs and execution.

## 10. Key risks

- **Crude supply risk:** feedstock availability and logistics can affect utilization.
- **Refining-margin normalization:** H1 2026 margins may not persist across a full market cycle.
- **Execution risk:** the remaining expansion programme requires substantial commissioning and delivery.
- **Funding risk:** approximately $11.8B of expansion spending remains in the disclosed programme.
- **FX risk:** a large operating and capital structure exposure to NGN/USD movements can affect translated economics and funding.
- **Tax transition:** tax treatment may change as domestic customs-territory sales and incentive conditions evolve.
- **Interest and debt cost:** financing costs can reduce earnings available to equity holders.
- **Product demand and pricing:** product prices and sales volumes affect revenue and margin capture.
- **Related-party transactions:** governance and disclosure should remain a continuing monitoring area.
- **Valuation multiple risk:** a lower market multiple applied to normalized earnings reduces implied equity value.

## 11. Final analytical view

The project identifies a clear operating turnaround but also shows why **₦525 is highly dependent on sustained earnings power**. The central variables to monitor are refining margin, utilization, cash generation, expansion execution and funding requirements.

The model's base scenario produces **₦446** versus the **₦525** IPO reference. The Strong and Bull scenarios exceed the offer reference, but require stronger sustainable earnings assumptions.

The appropriate use of this framework is therefore as a monitoring model: update the assumptions as new financial and operating disclosures arrive and test whether the earnings power supporting the valuation remains intact.
